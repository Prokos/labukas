## Next editorial work

Chapter 6 needs month names before declined dates; small study/work groups before broad profession lists; and prepared oral/written, repetition and slower-speech language before conversation. Isolate today's date, an event date and “in a month” with the same month/day. Teach relative event time separately from duration. Cover faculty/institution location, year of study, exam marks, word meanings, lateness, work availability and callbacks through actual functional tasks.

Then review chapters 7–10 for prerequisites, isolated grammar contrasts, natural translations, plausible choices, vocabulary load and spaced changed-context use. Review chapter 1 beyond its opening and the wider practice in chapters 3–5. Include weak-word trajectories and delayed retrieval when evaluating each chapter.

A chapter is ready for acceptance only when its required expressions and functions resolve to teaching, all response demands are prepared, hints and repairs preserve honest evidence, later returns cover its targets, and the rendered experience has been reviewed at desktop and phone widths. Teacher review and learner observation remain separate requirements for stronger claims about teaching quality.
