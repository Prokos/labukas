# Chapter 4 teaching review — 26 September 2026

Status: substantive revision started. This is an internal source-based review, not independent teacher approval. The current draft has 79 lessons; that count says nothing about how well it teaches.

## Findings before revision

- “Make yourself at home” introduces two room nouns and an on-the-table phrase in one group, while its rule also lists four prepositions and locative. The activity sequence does not isolate the claimed contrast.
- The early rental conversation appears at lesson 10, but the rent/room questions are formally introduced around lessons 68–69. Its distractors are not consistently prepared.
- A floor lesson abruptly switches to adjective agreement; another mixes no table, many windows and few chairs. Both change several grammatical dimensions together.
- Feminine numbers are rehearsed as isolated labels before plural formation or the same-number gender contrast is clearly taught.
- Permission versus requesting someone else’s action is not explicitly contrasted, despite being a central source function.
- Source pages 90–92 teach noun plural formation, genitive stem changes, count agreement, modal complements, spatial prepositions and adjective/question agreement. Mere presence of example phrases is insufficient coverage of these patterns.
- The previous-chapter bridge is chosen by array position, so adding chapter 3 content changes which arbitrary words return.

## Teaching contract

Start with the language needed to understand and ask about a flat: rooms, location, presence/absence, plural room counts, floor, internet and rent. Prepare both correct and competing replies before the rental exchange. Extend into descriptions, permission, help and household problems, then wider furnishings and spatial relations.

Each grammar lesson keeps the noun or frame stable while the important form changes. In particular: yra stalas / nėra stalo; du stalai / dvi kėdės; pirmas aukštas / pirmame aukšte; galiu atidaryti / galite atidaryti. Teach plural forms before requiring number agreement. Teach action vocabulary before combining it with modal verbs. Later checks change the surrounding context while preserving the actual target identity.

Keep source expressions traceable and existing saved attempts resumable. Retire only genuinely replaced draft lessons. Do not let new lesson count or passing interaction tests substitute for source-function coverage and an editorial review.

## Implemented revision

The functional route reaches a prepared four-turn rental enquiry at lesson 12. It teaches room names and locations, presence/absence, plural naming forms, number gender, room-count questions, floor locations, internet and rent first. The wider housing inventory follows that exchange.

Further authored teaching isolates ant/prie; one/three/five and ten/kiek; changed noun stems; noun needs versus infinitive needs; adjective/question gender; spatial relations; permission versus requests; obligation versus desire; hot-water problems; room numbers versus floors; and hosting. A two-advert reading requires combining facilities, room count and floor information. Source readings receive preparation for their repair/hosting chunks and a small attached glossary for advert wording.

Twenty-seven reserved recombinations supplement four final mixed checks. They retain the earlier target identities. Tests reject repeated model sentences and duplicate checks; copied scheduled returns are counted separately. A check initially presented as new was already modelled and was replaced during this review. Six redundant draft lessons are retired, while their saved queues remain resumable. The source ledger names actual replacement teaching. The chapter-3 bridge now deliberately retrieves a meeting point, day and time.
