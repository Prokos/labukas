# Chapter 2 quality audit

Review date: 25 September 2026. Scope: the replacement at `/?preview=chapter2`, not the original course or other chapters.

**Verdict: suitable for a serious learner review; not yet evidence of reliable independent café communication or complete chapter mastery.** Confidence is reasonably high in the breadth of text material, moderate in teaching recognition and familiar written patterns, and lower in transfer and durable retention. The implementation is substantially broader than the original 28-lesson draft, but more screens do not establish higher teaching quality.

This is a separate audit pass by the implementing assistant, checking source objectives and actual tasks independently of the coverage script's declarations. It is not an external Lithuanian educator's review or a learner study. The distinction matters when assessing confidence.

## Evidence and method

- Compared the supplied source chapter's vocabulary, numbers, dialogues, 14 grammar blocks, exercises, reading and writing (printed pages 31–62) with the authored models and response demands.
- Inspected the old chapter's 34 classes, 227 scheduled sessions, 302 content items and 134 unique core vocabulary entries. Checked lexical coverage separately from class-level objective mappings.
- Inspected prompts, English translations, noun/person contrasts, distractors, supports, later returns, reading questions and both final-conversation variants. Used wrong-form fixtures independent of the accepted-answer arrays.
- Tested the actual evidence trajectory: spelling versus wrong forms; correct after hint/reveal/visible correction; repeated same-day success; next-day success; midnight less than four hours apart; recognition without production; bounded mixed Practice; and serialization.
- Reviewed desktop/phone screenshots for the full answer field, grammar model, correction, menu, conversation, writing and progress. Browser automation verifies interactions and sequence completion, not learning.

## Reinforcement: sufficient for what?

The original concern about losing practice was justified. The first draft did omit active work on much of the lexical inventory. That is fixed: every inventory word is retrieved and revisited after an intervening lesson; early communication begins before the large inventory is finished.

The path now has 60 same-frame grammatical returns plus six explicitly changed-context grammatical returns in cumulative review. Contextual word gaps reuse preferences, descriptions and menu phrases. The final review lessons combine earlier vocabulary/forms with recent cultural vocabulary. That gives more than immediate recognition, but a gap with a known noun is still a narrow task. It does not establish that the learner can assemble the surrounding sentence independently.

Calendar spacing is supplied by Practice and mastery evidence, not by counting lessons. Completing the whole chapter today cannot count as remembered later. Recognition, visible corrections, requested help, spelling slips and self-reviewed writing cannot create full independent-recall credit. Successful later retrieval can clear difficulty; an old error does not impose a permanent penalty. Small one-target batches cannot provide genuine interleaving.

**Judgment:** the mechanism is credible for supporting retention, and the required returns close the clear coverage gap. Whether the amount is sufficient for this learner remains unproven. A next-day and later-week unaided check is needed; no retention percentage can honestly be estimated from these tests.

## Remaining weaknesses and release boundaries

1. **Transfer remains the main teaching weakness.** Many questions retrieve the same word/form in the same frame. The final conversation still supplies most replies as choices or tiles. It samples an exchange; it cannot certify spontaneous communication.
2. **Pacing is uneven.** Vocabulary lessons repeat a familiar model/recognition/matching/retrieval pattern, often over 20–28 screens. Some grammatical lessons introduce several endings in one model. Review with the learner should examine both fatigue and abrupt form demands, not simply add more lessons.
3. **Feedback is deterministic and bounded.** Unknown free wording is compared with an example, not confidently condemned. Accepted paraphrases and explicit spelling-versus-case contrasts are authored; they are not a general Lithuanian grammar checker. A qualified language review remains valuable before publication.
4. **Progress integration is incomplete outside the review build.** Chapter Course and Practice share assessment/mastery rules. Their saved events are separate from original course history; local backup/import now includes them and cross-chapter returns read their evidence. Original cloud sync and dashboard totals still do not include them; those remain production release blockers. The original history is preserved, not silently reinterpreted.
5. **Physical mobile keyboard behavior is not fully established.** Phone-sized browser checks and a reduced-height viewport pass, but do not replace testing real iOS/Android keyboards.
